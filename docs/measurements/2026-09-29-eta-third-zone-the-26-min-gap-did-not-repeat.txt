Yesterday the middle row looked like a fixed cost the model was not charging
for, flat across six hours, and the hypothesis recorded was that the travel
constants carry a DISTANCE term -- fitted on nearer zones, short on far ones.

THAT HYPOTHESIS IS NOT SUPPORTED. job_0098 ran on the same constants and scored
-0.7 minutes mean over 67 samples. If distance were the driver, the error would
track how far the zone sits from the docks; it does not.

What is left is simpler and less satisfying: zone 2 was slower than its ore
predicted. Per-zone variance, not a missing constant.

WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN THE GOOD NUMBER

Fitting a distance term to job_0092 was the obvious move yesterday and it would
have been wrong. It would have improved one zone and broken the two that were
already inside four minutes. The rule that saved it is the one already written
down after the averaged estimate: one job is not a basis for a constant. Two
zones now agree at |err| under 4 minutes on jobs of about 400; one sits 26 out.
Three zones is still not many, and the next zone that misses will be judged the
same way -- recorded, not tuned.

A GATE FALSE ALARM, FIXED

[2b] flagged job_0096 for mining one sector twice, 148 minutes apart. That was a
re-mine after a rescan -- the design working. The fault [2b] exists for was a
miner running its FIRST sector twice back to back off a spare copy of one order.
Time alone cannot separate them, because a rich sector legitimately takes three
hours. A rescan beginning between the two completions can, and now does. The
original fault is still caught, and re-mines are printed and counted rather than
silently dropped.

reads 0 miners left early over 2 mine jobs judged. The fault did not occur, so
the top-up has not been exercised. That is not evidence it works.
