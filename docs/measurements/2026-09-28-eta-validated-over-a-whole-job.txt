4. TWO PLACES WHERE THE NUMBER IS STILL NONSENSE, BOTH NOW FIXED.

   THE SURVEY PHASE READ 45 MINUTES FOR A 6h44m JOB.

     17:42  SURVEY   45.2 min      (402 min remained)
     17:57  SURVEY   89.3 min
     18:02  SURVEY  208.3 min
     18:07  MINE    380.0 min

   1.9.117 priced a sector the survey had not reached as an empty pass, and
   documented that as the honest choice because the ore was unknown. It is honest
   and it is useless: it told the operator forty-five minutes when the answer was
   nearly seven hours, and it climbed eightfold over twenty-five minutes. 1.9.119
   reverses it. An unscanned sector is priced from what THIS zone's scanned sectors
   turned out to hold, falling back to 4,600 ore -- the mean of the last two zones,
   4,454 and 4,743 -- until the first scan lands.

   This is not the averaging that the 1.9.116 failure warned about. That was
   averaging per-sector COST where each sector's ore was known, and it was wrong by
   13.5 minutes a sector. This is a prior on ORE where nothing whatever is known,
   and the survey replaces it with measurement within minutes.

   THE RESCAN SPIKED IT TO 96 MINUTES WITH 22 LEFT TO RUN.

     23:57  RESCAN   10.7 min
     00:02  MINE     95.8 min      (22 min remained)
     00:07  MINE      3.6 min

   When the rescan queues sectors for re-mining, they go back on the mine list --
   and sectorSeen deliberately keeps the LARGEST view a sector ever had, so a later
   partial scan cannot shrink it. So each re-mine sector was priced at its full
   original ore: 4,835 ore of digging on ground with a few dozen blocks left.
   Queued sectors are now priced by ore remaining, exactly as sectors in hand
   already were, through one shared helper.

5. WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED

   * One job, one zone, two miners, four sectors. The 14 minute flight home rests
     on four observations from two zones that are 300 blocks apart; a zone much
     further from the docks will pay more, and nothing here measures that.
   * The 4,600 ore prior is two zones wide. A barren or a much deeper zone will
     make the survey-phase estimate wrong again, in whichever direction it differs.
     It will still beat pricing that ground at zero.
   * The +16 minute worst case sits at the start of the mine phase, where the
     re-mine allowance is a guess about ore nobody has seen yet. That is the next
     thing worth measuring, and it needs several jobs, not one.
   * Log loss over this window was 0.2% (36 lines of 17,096), where the last three
     measurements read 0%. Not gating and not investigated. Recorded so that a
     later reading has something to compare against.
