ETA: THE SECTORS BEING MINED WERE INVISIBLE TO IT           W3, 2026-09-27
================================================================================

WHAT WAS MEASURED, AND WHY IT COUNTS AS A FAILURE

1.9.117 replaced an averaged per-sector ETA with one priced from the ore the
survey found. The per-sector formula fitted well (r=0.993, n=34) and I said at
the time that the honest test was not whether the number looked plausible but
whether it matched the clock. A recorder sampled the dashboard's own figure every
ten minutes through job_0086/0087 on 2026-09-24. It did not match the clock.

  when     phase     ETA said   ore out of the zone
  14:34    SURVEY      35.8 min      0
  14:54    SURVEY     273.8 min      0
  15:04    MINE       191.1 min    375
  15:14    MINE       191.1 min   1025
    ... unchanged, every sample, for two and a half hours ...
  17:34    MINE       191.1 min   8719
  17:44    MINE 1/4   105.7 min   9104

The jobs completed at 21:26:15 and 21:28:22. So at 15:04 it said 191 minutes
with 382 to go; at 17:34 it still said 191 with 232 to go; and at 17:44, as a
sector finished, the estimate FELL to 106 with 222 still to go.

The number never moved while 8,344 ore came out of the ground. That is the
finding: not that it was inaccurate, but that it was not watching the work.

THE CAUSE

jobEta priced zone.pending. Handing a sector to a miner pops it off pending.
So the sectors actually being mined -- the ones that decide when the job ends --
were priced at nothing, and the one list the estimate did watch could not change
while the miners dug. On a four-sector zone with two miners that is half the
remaining work, invisible.

REPLAY AGAINST THE REAL SECTOR DATA

The server's own completion lines give the zone exactly: four sectors, two
miners, one shared zone across job_0086 and job_0087.

  (1760,-3104)  4420 ore   done 17:43:45  node_119
  (1792,-3136)  5110 ore   done 18:06:38  node_138
  (1760,-3136)  4512 ore   done 20:59:26  node_138
  (1792,-3104)  3775 ore   done 21:00:58  node_119
  then a rescan and re-mine pass, 0 ore, and complete at 21:26:15.

Both models replayed over that zone, in minutes remaining:

     clock |  1.9.117 |  1.9.118 |  actual |  .117 err |  .118 err
  ---------+----------+----------+---------+-----------+----------
     15:04 |      177 |      344 |     382 |      -205 |      -38
     17:14 |      177 |      209 |     252 |       -75 |      -43
     17:44 |       90 |      185 |     222 |      -132 |      -37

(The replay's 1.9.117 column reads 177 where the live server read 191, because
the replay prices a sector by the ore actually recovered from it rather than by
the survey's own slightly larger view. That bias is in the same direction as the
error being measured, so it does not flatter the new model.)

WHAT 1.9.118 DOES

Prices the sectors in the miners' hands from zone.lastAssignments -- the holder
data popUnheld already trusts -- charging:
  * only the ore still in the ground, found minus mined, both of which the scan
    reports already file per sector, so the figure falls while a miner digs;
  * the setup overhead only while nothing has been reported out of the sector
    yet, which is exactly when the miner is still travelling, placing its loader
    and scanning;
  * a survey pass in hand at the full pass cost, because the scans that would
    price it are the thing it is out there producing.
The estimate is then the greater of the work spread over the miners and the
deepest single hold, which no other miner can take a share of.

Four tests, nine mutants. The one that matters is "the estimate falls as the ore
comes out of the sector in hand" -- the direct regression for the frozen 191.

WHAT IS STILL WRONG, STATED BEFORE ANYONE ELSE FINDS IT

The new model still reads LOW, by 38, 43 and 37 minutes at the three sample
points. A residual that is near-constant across two and a half hours is a fixed
cost that is not priced, not a rate that is wrong.

The visible candidate accounts for under half of it: the tail. The last ore
sector finished at 21:00:58 and the jobs completed at 21:26 and 21:28, so the
closing rescan and re-mine pass cost 25-27 minutes against an allowance of 17,
and the return to dock and the completion handshake are priced nowhere at all.

NOT TUNED, DELIBERATELY. This is one job. Adding a constant that makes n=1 fit
is how the averaged estimate got its authority in the first place. The
prediction instead: the residual will show up again, roughly constant, on the
next recorded job. If it does, it is a fixed tail cost and can be measured and
priced. If it scales with the job instead, the per-ore rate is low and the
constant would have been the wrong fix.

The recorder runs on the next job either way.
