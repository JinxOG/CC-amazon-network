This is the first zone run on 1.9.120, which added three things: a prior for
ground the survey has not reached, pricing re-mine sectors by ore remaining, and
a 14-minute charge for the trip home.

1. THE SURVEY-PHASE PRIOR WORKS.

   First reading of the job: 402 minutes, against 404 actually remaining. Two
   minutes out.

   The same moment under 1.9.117 read 45 minutes for a job of comparable length.
   That bug is gone.

   BUT THE TWO-MINUTE FIGURE IS PARTLY LUCK, and it would be dishonest to bank
   it. The prior assumes 4,600 ore a sector; this zone held 4,298. That
   overestimate happened to cancel the underestimate described in (3). Two errors
   in opposite directions is not accuracy, and on a richer zone they will add
   instead of cancel.

2. THE RESCAN SPIKE IS GONE.

   Through the rescan and re-mine passes the estimate read 44, 41, 40, then 35,
   32, 29, 25, 21 -- monotone to the end. Under 1.9.119 the same transition
   jumped from 11 minutes to 96 with 22 minutes left to run, because re-mine
   sectors were charged for ore already out of the ground. Fixed and confirmed.

3. A NEW GAP: THE MINE PHASE NOW READS 26 MINUTES LOW.

   65 mine-phase samples with over 40 minutes to run:

     mean error      -26.1 min
     worst error     -32.0 min
     mean |error|     26.1 min

   On the PREVIOUS zone, the same model with the trip home priced in scored
   +1.8 mean and 4.0 mean absolute. Same code, same constants, different ground.

   The error is flat across six hours, which is the signature of a fixed cost
   rather than a wrong rate -- the same signature that led to the trip home last
   time, and the trip home is already priced here.

   THE HYPOTHESIS, NOT YET TESTED: the overhead constants are distance-dependent.
   ETA_OVERHEAD_S (7.7 min of travel, loader placement and retrieval per sector)
   and ETA_RETURN_S (14 min home) were both fitted on zones around x 1760-2080,
   z -3136 to -2784. This zone sits further out. If travel dominates those
   constants, a zone twice as far pays roughly twice the travel on every sector
   and on the flight home, and the estimate reads low by a fixed amount -- which
   is exactly what 26 flat minutes looks like.

   NOT TUNED. Two zones is not a basis for a distance term, and adding one now
   would fit a line to two points. What is needed is the distance from the dock
   recorded alongside each job's error, over several more zones. The recorder
   already captures everything else; that is the next change to it.
