The live fleet's GPS stopped working for every turtle on 2026-10-29, and the
server's own GPS check still reports it healthy.

CAUSE: ESTABLISHED (operator, 2026-11-03). Nobody logged in for more than
seven days, so the chunks holding the GPS host computers unloaded and the hosts
stopped running. The turtles could not get a fix because there was nothing to
answer them. Logging back in reloaded those chunks and the failures stopped.

AND SECTION 4 BELOW WAS WRONG. It made much of an apparent contradiction --
"something answers the server and nothing answers the turtles" -- and there was
no contradiction. The /state readings quoted there were taken at 02:27Z and
05:32Z on 11-03, BOTH AFTER the last failure (before 01:30Z), so they describe
the world after the fix. What the server said DURING the outage is exactly what
unloaded hosts produce, and it said it every day:

  10-29 21:57  GPS CHECK FAILED: no_fix: 0 of 4 GPS hosts answered
  10-30 12:03  ...0 of 4...   10-30 19:55  ...0 of 4...   10-30 19:56  ...0 of 4...
  10-31 12:03  ...0 of 4...   11-01 13:03  ...0 of 4...   11-02 13:05  ...0 of 4...

Zero of four, consistently. The server and the turtles agreed throughout. The
error was mine: comparing a live reading against log lines from hours earlier
and calling the mismatch a finding. Compare like with like, or do not compare.

It also explains the detail flagged as odd below -- only about one check failure

4. AND THE SERVER DISAGREES

/state, read at 2026-11-03 02:27Z:

    gps = {"ok": true, "hosts": 4, "checkedAt": 1793672877871}

The server's own GPS check says four hosts answered it, within the minute. So
whatever is wrong, it is not "the hosts are switched off": something answers
the server computer and nothing answers the turtles. Two callers, two results.
That contradiction is the most useful thing here and it is why this note does
not offer a cause.

The server also logged, once on 10-29 at 21:57:55Z -- inside the ramp above --

    until GPS is back

and then once a day since, each time inside the daily restart window, which is
the ordinary case of the server checking before the hosts are up.

5. WHAT IT MEANS FOR WORK

"Dispatched job" lines: 0 on 10-28, 10-29, 10-30, 11-01 and 11-02. The fleet
has dispatched nothing for at least six days. Some of that is deliberate -- the
standing instruction since 2026-10-28 is test server only, no live deploys --
so the zero on 10-28 predates this fault and is not evidence of it. But the
server's own rule is "no job is sent out until GPS is back", so while this
holds, a mining order would not be dispatched even if one were given.

6. WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE FLIGHT-LANE CARD (W1)

The card "Miners lose GPS and freeze on one stretch of the flight lane
(x ~1300-1550, y 200, z -2813)" is framed as a coverage gap over one stretch of
sky. Two findings bear on it, in opposite directions:

  - Its own evidence can no longer be examined. /logs keeps 14 days
    (retentionDays: 14, earliest day on file 2026-10-21); the incidents it
    rests on are 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-16. Those lines are gone. The card's
    measurements have to be taken as written or re-gathered.
  - Its premise does not fit what is happening now. GPS locate is failing at
    the DOCK, for every turtle, continuously. A card about one stretch of lane
    cannot be worked on, or tested against the live system, while coverage is
    failing at the base too -- any lane measurement taken now would be
    measuring this instead.

The honest position is that the lane card is blocked on this, not that this
explains it: the two windows do not overlap, and in 10-14..10-16 the base had
GPS (112 successful resyncs a day as late as 10-28).
