A WHOLE-HOST OUTAGE IN THE MIDDLE OF A JOB                 W3, 2026-09-30
================================================================================

Unplanned. The Minecraft host went down at 16:48 UTC with job_0106/0107 half
done - two miners underground on a four-sector zone at x 1952..1984,
z -3296..-3264, 2 of 4 sectors mined, 4 of 4 surveyed. Nothing was dispatched
to cause it and nothing in our code failed. It was observed live on the way back
up, which is the part that cannot be reconstructed later.

THE TIMELINE (UTC)

  16:48:07  last line before the outage: a routine warehouse storage probe. The
            server's own health rollup seconds earlier was normal (busy avg
            4.6 ms, nothing slow). Both miners were scanning at Y=16.
  16:48 - 18:04   76 minutes of total silence. The bridge itself did not
            answer - the recorder logged connection timeouts every 5 min.
  18:04:17  bridge up. It restarted a second time around 18:11 (its 60 s
            rollups shift phase, and its uptime agrees).
  18:04 - 18:16   bridge up, pushes=0: Minecraft still loading the world.
  18:16:00  dispatch server boots, v1.9.121.
  18:16:07  all 17 machines re-registered and online within 20 seconds.
  18:16:08  last status from the two miners: RETRIEVING (boot recovery).
  18:16 - 18:33   the two miners fly home, reporting NOTHING.
  18:24:02  server declares both miners offline and gives their jobs to
            node_138 and node_139.
  18:33:32  both reappear at the bay approach point; then dock.

WHAT WORKED

  * NOTHING WAS LOST THAT WAS SAVED. 41 persistent zones reloaded from the cloud
    copy, both live jobs restored, and both zones came back at exactly 2/4
    mined, 4/4 surveyed - the last state before the outage.
  * The fleet was all back within 20 seconds of the server booting.
  * The server noticed both miners had rebooted mid-job and re-sent their jobs,
    held off new dispatch for 60 s so the fleet did not stampede, and when the
    jobs stalled it requeued them to idle miners, which resumed at "2 already
    done" - their finished sectors intact.
  * Both miners collected their chunk loaders and came home on their own.
    Nothing was stranded, and the operator confirmed it in the world.

WHAT FAILED, IN ORDER OF HOW MUCH IT MATTERS

1. A RECOVERING MINER GOES SILENT UNTIL IT REACHES THE BAYS.

   ROOT CAUSE, found in the code after this was first written: BY DESIGN. A
   turtle has two upgrade slots. After a crash nothing else is loading its
   chunks, so for the flight home it fits pickaxe + chunk loader and gives up
   the modem ("Chunk loading first, comms later"). Flying with the modem would
   risk freezing in an unloaded chunk - actually stranded. The silence is the
   price of that, and is NOT changed. The server's handling of it is: 1.9.122
   requeues the job the moment boot recovery is reported, keeps the turtle out
   of dispatch until it docks, and gives the silence the length of a flight
   rather than a 60 s loader swap.

   After the reboot each miner sent one status - RETRIEVING - and then nothing
   for 17 minutes while it climbed to its loader, collected it and flew about
   1,800 blocks home. Position, phase and fuel all froze on the dashboard at
   the loader, then jumped to the bay approach point on arrival. The operator
   has seen this after previous outages; it is a pattern, not a one-off.

   Proof it was a real, unreported flight and not a teleport or a stale map:
   fuel dropped ~2,700 on each turtle between the frozen reading and the first
   one after arrival (99,579 -> 96,884 and 85,269 -> 82,585), and the operator
   saw them docked. While the fuel field was frozen it read as "zero fuel
   burned over 1,770 blocks", which briefly suggested a bogus position. It was
   a frozen field, not a frozen turtle.

   THE CONSEQUENCE IS NOT COSMETIC. At 18:24, midway home, the server timed
   both out as offline and gave their jobs to node_138 and node_139. They were
   alive. They docked still holding job_0106 and job_0107 - jobs another turtle
   now owns. After every outage the server and the recovering turtles disagree
   about who is doing what.

   Checked, not assumed: the idle-stuck watchdog, which requeues a job whose
   turtle is IDLE, looks the turtle up by job.assignedTo - so it inspects
   node_138 for job_0106, never the stale node_118. The stale IDs cannot make it
   yank a live job. They are untidy, not dangerous, as far as that path goes.

   Note the server's first move at 18:16:08 was to re-send each miner its job;
   the miner's boot recovery did something else (retrieve and go home). Two
   state machines, two answers - the deferred Problem B in the architecture
   notes, seen live.

2. CORRECTED - THE RECOVERY LOGS WERE NOT LOST. THE FLIGHT HAD NONE.

   First written here as "boot recovery ships no log at all". WRONG, and
   corrected the same evening. node_118 shipped 45 lines and node_119 44, zero
   missing: held on the turtle while its modem was off and delivered on docking,
   stamped with their original times. They were simply not there yet when the
   bridge was first checked mid-flight.

   What IS missing is narrower: the FLIGHT ITSELF. node_118's own log reads
   "Loader retrieved; self-loading restored" at 18:18:10 and then nothing until
   18:33:06 at the arrivals hole. The boot-recovery code already had a legReport
   for each leg of that flight, but it reported only by radio, on a flight flown
   with the radio off, so it never fired on the path it was written for.
   Fixed in 1.9.122: legReport now also writes to the turtle's own log.

3. THE BOOT CRASH REPORT QUOTES A CRASH FROM 24 DAYS AGO.

   On boot: "Restarted after a crash - last: [2026-09-06 01:39:16]
   server.run crashed: Terminated". Today's outage recorded no crash - the host
   was stopped from outside, the server never got to write one - so the line
   quoted the newest entry in crash.log, from September 6th. The identical line
   appeared after the 1.9.121 deploy on 09-29, which was not a crash either.
   It fires on every restart and names the same month-old event whether it was
   a deploy, a power cut or a real crash.

   What was needed - "stopped abruptly around 16:48, down 88 min, nothing
   recorded" - had to be rebuilt by hand from the log. A periodic "still alive"
   timestamp on disk, compared at boot against the newest crash entry, would
   say it directly.

4. MID-SECTOR ORE PROGRESS IS NOT SAVED (NOT fixed in 1.9.122 - see its card).

   Mined ore read 14,528 before the outage and 10,953 after: 3,575 of the
   server's accounting did not survive. The blocks were dug; the record is
   short. Cause, in code: the SECTOR_SCAN handler accumulates each sector's
   mined ore in memory, and the zone store is saved only at sector boundaries
   (assignment, completion). A stop partway through a two-hour sector loses the
   whole sector's progress. The estimate then read 219 min instead of ~150,
   because the model believes that ore is still in the ground, and the zone's
   final ore total will read short.

WHAT THIS COST, AND WHAT IT DID NOT

  Cost: 88 minutes of downtime, a second 1,800-block trip for each of two
  replacement miners, a dashboard blind for 17 minutes, 3,575 ore of
  accounting, and an ETA wrong by about an hour.
  Not lost: no turtle, no loader, no finished sector, no zone, no job.

INSTRUMENTS, AND WHAT THEY TAUGHT ABOUT THEMSELVES

  * The ETA recorder survived the outage, logging timeouts throughout, then
    died when the engineer's session ended - one minute before the bridge came
    back. Instruments tied to a session's lifetime miss exactly the recoveries
    that happen while nobody is watching.
  * The first attempt to restart it ran from the operator's own checkout, which
    is days behind and has no recorder. Run tools from the engineer's worktree.
  * /state has no "position" field; coordinates are top-level x/y/z, and the
    "locations" map is empty. A query against the wrong field reads as "every
    turtle has no position" - an idle turtle used as a control caught it.
  * /state is ~340 KB now; one read came back truncated mid-string.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE SCHEDULED DOWNTIME (~5 h after this was written)

  Park the fleet before it. The server, its records and idle turtles come back
  cleanly; a miner caught mid-job abandons its job, flies home unreported, and
  is reassigned while alive. That is survivable - today proved it - but it
  costs a round trip and leaves the registry wrong until something clears it.
